INTERVIEWER: Tell me about growing up in the neighborhood.

SPEAKER: We lived on Desire Street, right off MLK Boulevard, in New Orleans. My father James ran a repair shop out of the garage, but everyone knew him as Big Jim. Nobody called him James except the bank. Big Jim could fix anything. My mama Ruby kept the books and kept us all in line. She was the real boss.

INTERVIEWER: Big family?

SPEAKER: Huge. I have my twin brothers, Caleb and Josh, two years younger than me and identical, still can't tell them apart on the phone. My cousin Deborah practically lived with us; we call her Debbie. Debbie and I are the same age, born back in June 1975, so we came up like twins ourselves.

INTERVIEWER: What was the shop like?

SPEAKER: Loud, greasy, wonderful. Jim taught all of us to work with our hands. Caleb took it over eventually. Josh went a different way, into music, moved out to Lafayette. Every year, right before Christmas, Jim would fix up bikes for the kids on the block for free. That was Big Jim. Folks came from all over Desire Street.

INTERVIEWER: When did things change?

SPEAKER: When Katrina hit. We lost the shop, the house, everything on MLK Boulevard. Ruby held us together through it. A couple years ago we finally got a plaque put up where the shop used to be. Caleb pushed for that. I'm in my forties now and I still drive past that lot.

INTERVIEWER: Did you marry, have kids of your own?

SPEAKER: I did. My wife's father, Mr. Boudreaux, gave me my first real job after the storm, before we were even family, which is a funny story. He didn't trust me at first. Now he brags about me to anyone who'll listen. Funny how that goes.

INTERVIEWER: Any brushes with the famous?

SPEAKER: Ha. We're related to Governor Edwards, distantly, or so my mama always claimed. Nobody's ever proven it. Jim loved Elvis, played him on Sundays so loud the neighbors complained. So the closest we got to famous was Big Jim doing his Elvis impression in the driveway.

INTERVIEWER: How old is your father now?

SPEAKER: Seventy-three and still ornery. He moved in with Caleb after Ruby passed. He won't admit he needs help, classic Jim. The twins take turns checking on him. Deborah does too, she never left, unlike Josh who only comes home for holidays.

INTERVIEWER: What do you want people to know about your family?

SPEAKER: That we made something out of a garage on Desire Street. That Ruby and Big Jim built a life with their hands and their name meant something in that neighborhood. Whatever happens to me, I want my kids to carry that. We were people who showed up. That's the whole story, really.

INTERVIEWER: Thank you.

SPEAKER: My pleasure. Tell it right, now.
