INTERVIEWER: Thanks for sitting down with me. Maybe start with where you grew up?

SPEAKER: Sure. I was born in New Orleans, over in the Ninth Ward, in March 1990. My mom raised me mostly on her own. Her name was Gloria, but honestly everybody just called her Glo, even the teachers. She worked nights, so my aunt Maria watched us a lot of the time. Maria was my mom's older sister, and she basically lived at our place. Her husband, Ronnie, worked down at Charity Hospital as an orderly for years, and he'd bring home these stories.

INTERVIEWER: What was the neighborhood like?

SPEAKER: Close-knit. There was a woman down the block, Miss Rosa, who watched half the kids on the street. I still don't know her last name, we all just called her Miss Rosa. And there was a guy named Tank, big dude, who ran the corner store. His real name was Terrence but nobody used it. Tank looked out for us.

INTERVIEWER: Did your family stay in the city?

SPEAKER: Most of us, until the storm. Right after Hurricane Katrina we scattered. My cousin Denise ended up in Houston and never came back. Maria and Ronnie went to Baton Rouge for about a year, then came home. I was about nineteen then, so it hit during a weird time for me. A few years back I finally moved out to the suburbs myself, but the Ninth Ward is home.

INTERVIEWER: You mentioned teachers. Anyone stand out?

SPEAKER: My teacher Mr. Alvarez, he taught history. He's the one who got me reading. And later my counselor, Ms. Patterson, pushed me to apply to college when nobody else did. I owe her a lot. Funny enough, my best friend from back then, we've known each other since we were little, her name is Beyonce, no relation to the famous one, everyone teased her about it. She's a nurse now.

INTERVIEWER: Do you follow politics much?

SPEAKER: Some. I remember the year Obama got elected, the whole block was out celebrating. I even shook hands with Mayor Landrieu at a community event once, which felt like a big deal at the time. But I'm not really into all that.

INTERVIEWER: And now? Family of your own?

SPEAKER: Yeah. My wife, Carla, and I have two kids. My daughter Sofia just turned eight, and the little one, we call him Junior, he's three. Carla's mom, Rosa, lives with us now, different Rosa from Miss Rosa, I know it's confusing. I'm in my early thirties now, trying to give them what my mom gave me. My brother Marcus lives close too, so the kids see their uncle a lot. We do Sunday dinners like Gloria used to.

INTERVIEWER: That's beautiful. Thank you.

SPEAKER: Thank you for listening. It's good to talk about them.
